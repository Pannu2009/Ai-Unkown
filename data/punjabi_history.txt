Punjabi history.

Punjabi is one of the oldest living languages of South Asia. It grew out of the Shauraseni Prakrit around the 7th century. The name Punjab means land of five rivers: Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, and Sutlej.

The earliest Punjabi literature comes from the Nath yogis around the 10th century. Baba Farid, in the 12th century, wrote Punjabi poetry that later entered the Guru Granth Sahib Ji.

Guru Nanak wrote in Punjabi in the 1500s. Guru Angad Dev Ji formalized the Gurmukhi script, giving Punjabi its own writing system. The Sikh Gurus made Punjabi a literary language.

In the 1700s and 1800s, Punjabi Sufi poets like Bulleh Shah, Waris Shah, and Shah Hussain wrote masterpieces. Waris Shah's Heer Ranjha is the great Punjabi epic romance.

Maharaja Ranjit Singh made Lahore the capital of the Sikh Empire in 1799. Punjabi was the court language. The empire stretched from the Khyber to Kashmir.

In 1947, Punjab was divided between India and Pakistan. Millions crossed the new border. Punjabi became the language of a divided homeland.

Today Punjabi is spoken by over 125 million people. It is the 10th most spoken language in the world. It is official in Indian Punjab and widely spoken in Pakistani Punjab, Delhi, and the diaspora in Canada, the UK, and the US.

Punjabi is a tonal language. The same word said with a different tone means different things. This is rare among Indo-European languages.

Bhangra music took Punjabi around the world. Punjabi food, from sarson da saag to butter chicken, is famous everywhere.

Famous Punjabi phrases: Sat Sri Akaal (greeting), Chak De Phatte (go for it), Balle Balle (hooray), Ki haal hai (how are you).
