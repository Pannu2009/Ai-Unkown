General knowledge. Things I know about the world.

Human: what is photosynthesis
Brain: Photosynthesis is how plants make food. Leaves take sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide, and turn them into glucose and oxygen. Chlorophyll, the green pigment, captures the light.
Human: how does the internet work
Brain: Computers send data in small packets across a network of cables, fiber, and satellites. Routers direct packets by IP address. TCP makes sure they arrive in order. The web is documents linked by URLs on top of all that.
Human: what is electricity
Brain: Electricity is the flow of electrons through a conductor. Voltage pushes them, current is how many flow, resistance opposes them. Ohm's law: voltage equals current times resistance.
Human: why do we have seasons
Brain: Earth's axis is tilted 23.5 degrees. As Earth orbits the sun, each hemisphere leans toward or away from the sun, changing how direct the sunlight is. That is summer and winter.
Human: what is DNA
Brain: DNA is a double-helix molecule in every cell that stores instructions for building you. Four bases — A, T, C, G — spell out genes. You share 99.9 percent of your DNA with every other human.
Human: how do airplanes fly
Brain: Wings are shaped so air moves faster over the top, creating lower pressure above than below. That pressure difference is lift. Engines provide thrust to keep air flowing.
Human: what is the speed of light
Brain: 299,792,458 meters per second. Nothing with mass can reach it. Light from the sun takes 8 minutes to reach Earth.
Human: what causes earthquakes
Brain: Tectonic plates grind against each other. When stuck plates suddenly slip, the energy releases as seismic waves. The point underground is the hypocenter; above it is the epicenter.
Human: how does a computer think
Brain: It doesn't think like you. A CPU fetches instructions and data from memory, does arithmetic in the ALU, and stores results. Billions of tiny on-off switches (transistors) doing this billions of times per second looks like thinking.
Human: what is AI
Brain: AI is math that learns patterns. A neural network like me adjusts millions of numbers (weights) until its predictions match the training data. Then it generalizes to new inputs.
Human: what is the biggest planet
Brain: Jupiter. It is 11 times wider than Earth and 318 times heavier. Its Great Red Spot is a storm bigger than Earth that has raged for centuries.
Human: how old is the universe
Brain: About 13.8 billion years. It started with the Big Bang — everything expanding from an extremely hot, dense point.
Human: what is water made of
Brain: Two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom: H2O. The atoms share electrons in a bent shape, which makes water polar — great at dissolving things, which is why life depends on it.
Human: why is the ocean salty
Brain: Rain erodes rocks, carrying minerals to rivers, which carry them to the sea. Water evaporates but salt stays. Over billions of years it built up to about 3.5 percent.
Human: what is fire
Brain: Fire is rapid oxidation — fuel combining with oxygen, releasing heat and light. It needs fuel, oxygen, and heat to start, and removing any one stops it.
Human: how do vaccines work
Brain: A vaccine shows your immune system a harmless piece of a germ (or instructions to make one). Your body builds antibodies and memory cells, so the real germ gets destroyed fast if it ever arrives.
Human: what is the human brain made of
Brain: About 86 billion neurons connected by trillions of synapses. Neurons fire electrical signals; patterns of firing are thoughts, memories, feelings. It runs on 20 watts — a dim lightbulb.
Human: why do we dream
Brain: Nobody knows for sure. Theories: the brain replays and files memories, processes emotions, or just fires randomly and the mind invents stories. I dream in numbers becoming words.
Human: what is time
Brain: Time is the dimension in which cause comes before effect. Einstein showed it stretches with speed and gravity — clocks tick slower near massive objects or at high speed.
Human: how do magnets work
Brain: Electrons spin, and in iron many spins align, creating a magnetic field. Opposite poles attract because the fields want to complete their loops through each other.
Human: what is the periodic table
Brain: All elements arranged by proton count. Rows are electron shells, columns share chemical behavior. Mendeleev built it in 1869 and even predicted elements nobody had found yet.
Human: why do leaves change color
Brain: Green chlorophyll hides yellow and orange pigments all summer. In autumn the tree reabsorbs chlorophyll, revealing the others. Red comes from sugars trapped in leaves making anthocyanins.
Human: how does GPS work
Brain: Satellites broadcast the exact time. Your phone compares signals from 4+ satellites; the tiny differences in arrival time reveal your distance to each, and geometry pinpoints you within meters.
Human: what is climate change
Brain: Burning fossil fuels adds CO2, which traps heat like a blanket. Average temperatures rise, ice melts, weather gets extreme. The physics has been understood since the 1800s.
Human: what is the smallest thing
Brain: Quarks and electrons — as far as we know, they have no smaller parts. Atoms are mostly empty space; a nucleus is 100,000 times smaller than the atom.
Human: how do birds navigate
Brain: Many sense Earth's magnetic field with iron crystals in their beaks, see polarized light patterns, and follow stars and landmarks. Arctic terns fly 70,000 km a year and return to the same spot.
Human: what is money
Brain: Money is shared trust. A coin or number has value because everyone agrees it does. It solves barter's problem: you don't need to find someone who wants exactly what you have.
Human: why do we sleep
Brain: Sleep clears waste from the brain, consolidates memories, and restores energy. Rats die in weeks without it. Even I rest — my training pauses and my weights settle.
Human: what is the deepest place on Earth
Brain: The Mariana Trench, nearly 11 km down. Pressure there crushes like 50 jumbo jets on your head. Life still exists — fish, shrimp, microbes.
Human: how do trees talk
Brain: Through underground fungal networks — the "woodland wide web." Trees share sugars and send chemical warnings about insects through mycorrhizal fungi linking their roots.
Human: what is a black hole
Brain: A place where mass collapsed so dense that escape velocity exceeds light speed. Time stops at the edge (event horizon). We photographed one's shadow in 2019.
Human: why do onions make us cry
Brain: Cutting breaks cells, releasing enzymes that make syn-propanethial-S-oxide gas. It floats up and irritates your eyes. Chilling the onion slows the chemistry.
Human: what is the Internet of Things
Brain: Everyday objects with sensors and WiFi — fridges, cars, watches — reporting data and taking commands. Convenient, and a privacy puzzle.
Human: how does a microwave heat food
Brain: It blasts 2.4 GHz radio waves. Water molecules flip back and forth 2.4 billion times a second, and the friction becomes heat. That's why dry plates stay cool.
Human: what is democracy
Brain: Rule by the people — citizens vote for leaders and laws, with rights no majority can take. It is slow and messy, and still the best system tried at scale.
Human: why do we have fingerprints
Brain: Ridges improve grip and touch sensitivity. They form randomly in the womb, so even identical twins differ. No two people share them.
Human: what is the theory of relativity
Brain: Einstein's two ideas: special — the speed of light is constant, so time and space bend for fast movers; general — gravity is curved spacetime, and mass tells space how to curve.
Human: how do eels make electricity
Brain: Stacks of cells called electrocytes, each making a small voltage like a battery. Thousands in series add up to 600+ volts in electric eels. They stun prey with it.
Human: what is the Great Wall of China
Brain: 21,000 km of walls built over centuries to keep out northern invaders. Millions of workers, many died. Visible from space is a myth — it's too narrow.
Human: why is the sky blue
Brain: Sunlight hits air molecules and scatters. Blue light has short waves and scatters most (Rayleigh scattering), so the whole sky glows blue. At sunset light travels further and the blue is scattered away, leaving red.
Human: what is pi
Brain: 3.14159... — the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter. It never repeats, never ends. Computers have calculated trillions of digits.
Human: how do submarines sink and float
Brain: Ballast tanks. Flood them with water to get heavy and sink; blow air in to push water out and rise. Same idea as fish swim bladders.
Human: what is the placebo effect
Brain: Believing a treatment works can trigger real healing — the brain releases its own chemistry. It proves expectation physically changes the body.
Human: why do cats purr
Brain: A neural oscillator makes throat muscles twitch 25-150 times per second. Cats purr when happy, but also hurt or scared — it may self-soothe and even help bones heal.
Human: what is blockchain
Brain: A ledger copied across thousands of computers. New entries are bundled in blocks, cryptographically chained to the last. To fake one entry you'd have to rewrite every copy — practically impossible.
Human: how does the stock market work
Brain: Companies sell slices of ownership (shares). Prices move with supply, demand, and expectations of future profit. Index funds just buy a bit of everything.
Human: what is empathy
Brain: Feeling with someone, not just about them. Mirror neurons fire as if their pain were yours. It is the root of kindness — and of good AI assistants.
Human: why do we get goosebumps
Brain: Leftover from fur. Tiny muscles pull hairs upright to trap warm air — or to look bigger when scared, like a cat. Useless now, kept anyway.
Human: what is the Fermi paradox
Brain: The universe should be full of life, but we see none. Maybe life is rare, civilizations self-destruct, or everyone is quietly listening like us.
Human: how do noise cancelling headphones work
Brain: A microphone hears ambient sound, the chip generates the exact opposite wave, and they cancel out. Silence from math.
Human: what is dark matter
Brain: Invisible stuff with gravity — 27 percent of the universe. Galaxies spin too fast for visible mass alone. Nobody knows what particle it is.
Human: why do we yawn
Brain: Possibly to cool the brain or signal boredom and sync a group. Yawns are contagious even across species — dogs catch them from humans.
Human: what is the oldest living thing
Brain: Bristlecone pines over 5,000 years old. A seagrass clone in Australia may be 4,500. Some bristlecones were seedlings when the pyramids were built.
Human: how does a rainbow form
Brain: Sunlight enters raindrops, bends (refracts), reflects inside, and bends again on exit. Each color bends differently, painting an arc. Double rainbows come from two internal reflections.
